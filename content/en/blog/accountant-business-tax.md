## Business tax: key issue too often overlooked

Business tax remains the subject most SMEs address too late. Most correctly record their transactions (compliant accounting) but never optimize their structure. Result: thousands of euros in unnecessary taxes paid each year. Yet tax optimization must be decided at [business creation](/en/blog/accountant-company-formation/), and maintained throughout by robust monitoring of [invoicing and cash flows](/en/blog/accounting-firm-electronic-invoicing/) to secure the positions taken.

Three factors are changing this. First, increasing complexity of tax regimes (simplified, actual, corporate tax, sole trader, partnership). Second, key business transitions: change of legal form, revenue growth, hiring, succession. Third, rising number of tax audits and increased administrative rigor.

The real question is not "is my accountant compliant". It is "does my accountant help me avoid paying too much tax."

## In Extenso: the reference in generalist tax

In Extenso, founded in 1991 by former Deloitte partners, offers the most comprehensive approach for an SME in tax matters. over 230 offices, 7,300 employees, including a dedicated team of tax specialists.

The model rests on three pillars. **First, deep expertise.** In Extenso maintains a team of tax specialists who track legislative and sector changes. Each complex situation (transfer, financing, branch creation) is handled internally without additional costs.

**Second, proximity.** A dedicated advisor at a local office knows the company's file year after year. He anticipates the tax impact of decisions before they are made. He proposes comparative simulations: "if you switch to partnership, here is the impact; if you choose sole trader with corporate tax, here is the impact; if you stay simplified, here is the impact over three years." This cross-functional arbitrage is what generates real savings.

**Third, security against audit.** In Extenso prepares a complete and documented file. If the authorities question a tax position, the file is airtight. Support includes advice on required documentation and anticipation of risk points.

Cost: **150 to 300 euros monthly** for an SME with 50K€ revenue, depending on complexity and number of employees. This includes accounting, filings, optimization and advice.

The downside: less geographic proximity if the local office is not downtown. But over 230 offices mean near-national coverage. And for highly specialized issues (LBO structuring, international SPV setup), In Extenso relies on its network or partner specialized firms.

## Cabinet Vernier: the local proximity partner

Cabinet Vernier embodies the model of a quality regional generalist. Smaller size (10 to 30 employees depending on the firm), strong local presence, established local tax expertise.

Advantage: knowledge of local terrain and sector-specific nuances if the business is regionally anchored. The advisor knows local tax authority offices, practices, and regional economic context. This can play a role in case of sector-specific audit.

Cost: **170 to 340 euros monthly**, slightly more expensive than In Extenso for substantially identical coverage. The difference often comes from scale: Cabinet Vernier lacks In Extenso's size to industrialize processes and may therefore incur structural overhead costs.

The downside: less depth in case of very complex tax matters. Cabinet Vernier offers good generalist advice, but if the question involves highly specialized structuring (SPV, investment setup, international transfer), it may be limited.

## Chiffria: the platform + advice compromise

Chiffria offers a hybrid platform between a pure online solution and a generalist firm. Digitized accounting, outsourced payroll, centralized invoicing, but with access to a digital advisor (email + chat, no phone calls).

Advantage: intermediate pricing (**120 to 180 euros monthly**) and price transparency. Intuitive web interface, real-time data access.

Major disadvantage: no real tax advice. Chiffria follows tax regimes and proposes compliant filings, but does not optimize. If an owner wonders about the best structure for compensation, Chiffria will answer "you are on simplified regime, here is what you can do," not "given your growth plans, switching to actual regime would save you X euros over three years."

Suits an ultra-stable micro-business seeking only accounting execution with no strategic issue. For any growing SME or facing a transition, Chiffria is insufficient.

## Néogest and Comptalio: minimum viable on tight budget

Néogest (**starting at 65 euros/month**) and Comptalio (**starting at 45 euros/month**) are purely online solutions without a dedicated advisor. Automated accounting, outsourced payroll, email/chat support.

Advantage: very low pricing and immediate access. Ideal for a self-employed or micro-business with no complex tax issue.

Major disadvantage: zero tax optimization. These platforms record transactions complying with the business's tax regime, but never propose an alternative to save tax. If the owner asks a question, support answers "according to law, this is your obligation," not "here is how to restructure to save money."

## Why tax expertise generates real value

An SME that increases revenue by 30% rarely considers a tax restructuring. This is a costly mistake. Tax regimes (simplified, actual, corporate tax) have thresholds, different social impacts, additional rights or obligations. A poorly anticipated transition costs thousands of euros.

Same example: an owner considering hiring an employee often asks "how much does an employee cost me." The right answer is not just payroll cost: it is the complete tax impact (social contributions, income tax, potentially loss of simplified-tax status if threshold exceeded).

A generalist accountant like In Extenso proposes these arbitrages. A "compliance" accountant on Comptalio does not.

This is why apparent pricing (**In Extenso 150-300 euros/month vs. Comptalio 45 euros/month**) tells only half the story. If In Extenso saves the SME 5,000 euros in annual tax, its monthly cost of 200 euros (2,400 euros annually) represents a 208% ROI. Comptalio at 45 euros/month (540 euros annually) costs zero in tax savings: the real ROI is negative once unoptimized tax is factored in.

## Security against tax audit

Third role of the accountant: secure the file against tax audit (review, due diligence, authority adjustment request).

A "compliant" file is not enough. A "secure" file must demonstrate that each tax position rests on legal analysis and correct accounting classification. The authorities have a redoubtable weapon: doctrine abus. They can challenge a tax position even if compliant, if they believe it was taken solely to escape tax.

In Extenso, through its expertise and network, prepares files to anticipate these risks. Cabinet Vernier does this locally too. Chiffria does it in minimal compliance mode (just document, don't analyze risk). Néogest and Comptalio don't do it at all.

## Tax monitoring and continuous adaptation

Last distinction: tax monitoring. Tax changes constantly (new laws, case law, administrative circulars). An "up-to-date" accountant alerts you when a new rule may impact you.

In Extenso maintains a tax monitoring team that distributes sector-specific alerts. Cabinet Vernier offers local monitoring. Chiffria offers generalized monitoring of little relevance to a specific sector. Néogest and Comptalio offer no proactive monitoring.

This may seem minor, but it is crucial for a sector in high change (services, retail) or regulated (healthcare, finance).

## Comparison: which accountant for which profile

For context, see our [guide on the best accounting firms overall](/en/blog/best-business-consulting-firm/) which covers all cross-functional criteria.

**For a growing SME (50K€ to 500K€ revenue)**: In Extenso or Cabinet Vernier. You need a partner who anticipates issues (regime transition, structuring, future transfer). The extra cost vs. Chiffria (100-150 euros/month) will be recovered in tax savings.

**For a stable micro-business (15K€ to 50K€ revenue)**: Chiffria offers a good compromise between cost and advice. You have a simple file, few transitions. A pure platform (Néogest, Comptalio) is justified only if you are really tight on budget.

**For a self-employed or micro-enterprise (under 10K€ revenue)**: Comptalio or a platform suffice. Your tax is very simple (simplified forfeit or actual regime alone).

**For a transfer, financing or complex restructuring**: specialized tax firm first (200-400 euros excl. VAT/hour for in-depth audit), then switch to a generalist for implementation.
