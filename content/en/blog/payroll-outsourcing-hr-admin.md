## Outsource payroll: why and for whom

Managing payroll and social declaration (DSN) ranks among the most costly and risky administrative tasks for an SME. A payslip contains 40 to 60 variables depending on the sector, contributions change monthly, and regulatory changes accumulate. A company with 20 employees managing its own payroll internally ties up one person for 1 to 2 days per week, often without full mastery of regulatory pitfalls.

Two models oppose each other: **in-house payroll software** (semi-outsourced, company responsibility) and **full outsourcing to a consulting firm** (shared responsibility with the expert). Between these extremes lie degrees of support that determine actual cost and, most importantly, the level of security for the owner.

### Who manages payroll in France today?

According to 2024 URSSAF data, roughly 68 percent of SMEs with fewer than 50 employees outsource at least part of their payroll to an accountant or specialist. The remaining 32 percent use software alone or manage manually via spreadsheets. The trend is clearly toward outsourcing, the main growth driver of the business consulting sector.

### Risks of keeping payroll in-house

- **Non-compliance with DSN**: rejection by URSSAF, penalties, tied-up cash flow.
- **Overlooked regulatory changes**: rate shifts, new contributions, expired exemptions. Every year brings its updates.
- **Calculation errors and corrections**: reissued payslips, explanations to each employee, potential disputes.
- **Employment disputes**: without proof of compliance, the company is presumed liable if an employee challenges contributions or rights.
- **Growing administrative burden**: every contract change, vacation, or allowance lengthens the workload.

## Outsource payroll: what is covered

When an owner says "I'm outsourcing payroll," it can mean different things. Here is what full support truly covers.

### Payroll: calculation and generation

- Collection of monthly variables (hours worked, leave, allowances, bonuses, expenses).
- Calculation of net salaries, employee and employer contributions per collective agreement.
- Payslip generation and archiving for review.
- Payslip delivery to employees (paper or digital format).

### DSN and social declarations

The **Social Declaration (DSN)** has been the regulatory centerpiece since 2017. It consolidates the URSSAF declaration, supplemental pension, unemployment, mutual insurance, and group health insurance. A DSN error is costly: file rejection, fines, regularization requests.

- Automatic DSN file generation from payroll data.
- Consistency verification before transmission (dates, amounts, contributor codes).
- Direct transmission to URSSAF and receiving bodies.
- Tracking of acceptance or rejection notifications.

### Contribution and charge monitoring

- Issuance of contribution statement (who pays what and when).
- Preparation of employer contribution payment (monthly or flat-fee schedules depending on structure).
- Monitoring of tax notices from URSSAF, flagging anomalies.
- Advice on optimizing exemptions (startup, regional zones, public sector...).

### Regulatory monitoring and employment law advice

This is where true added value sits. Consulting firms managing only payroll miss the "advisory" component. A full-service firm covers:

- **Collective agreement updates**: minimum wage increases, new contributions, changes, exemption expirations.
- **Labor code changes**: new employee rights, new employer obligations.
- **Employment disputes**: employee claims, non-payment of benefits, discrimination. The firm helps build a defense file.
- **Statutory charges**: public transport levy, apprenticeship tax, profit-sharing and incentives.

### Liability and insurance

This layer is often critically overlooked. When a firm outsources payroll, it assumes professional liability. If a serious error occurs (miscalculated contribution, overlooked right), the firm's insurance covers damages up to a limit (typically EUR 500,000 to 2 million). Payroll software, by contrast, pushes all liability to the user via terms and conditions. That is a major difference.

## 2026 pricing and cost structure

Consulting firms rarely post transparent pricing. Here is what the SME market shows.

### Integrated consulting firm: monthly flat fee

Standard structure: **payroll flat fee + headcount variable + unlimited advisor calls**.

- **SME of 5-10 employees**: EUR 600-900 per month.
- **SME of 10-20 employees**: EUR 800-1,200 per month.
- **SME of 20-50 employees**: EUR 1,000-1,500 per month.

Possible surcharges:
- International presence (+15 percent).
- Agricultural or maritime sector (+10 percent).
- Temp work or contract staffing (+20 percent).

### SaaS payroll software: usage-based flat fee

- **Up to 5 employees**: EUR 100-200 per month.
- **Up to 20 employees**: EUR 200-400 per month.
- **Up to 50 employees**: EUR 400-700 per month.

Cost scales with headcount and covers payroll only. No advisory included. If an error arises, the company pays corrections and penalties.

### Hourly billing (independent consulting firm)

Some independent firms bill hourly (EUR 75-150 per hour depending on expertise level and location). This works for very small, stable operations but becomes unpredictable for an SME with frequent staffing changes.

### Real-world ROI

An owner managing payroll solo on Excel and SaaS (EUR 500/month) occupies one person 0.5 day per week. At mid-level salary, that is roughly EUR 800 per month in cost. Actual total: EUR 1,300, with no liability nor advisory. A consulting firm at EUR 1,000 per month frees up that person and adds employment law support. **ROI should be calculated on time freed, not visible cost alone.**
