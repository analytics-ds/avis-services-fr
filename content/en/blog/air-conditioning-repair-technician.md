> **In brief:**
> 1. **Four channels can be used to find an air conditioning repair technician**: the local independent tradesperson, matchmaking platforms, the manufacturer's approved service network (Daikin, Mitsubishi Electric, Atlantic) and integrated companies such as **Murfy**.
> 2. **Any work on an air conditioning circuit containing refrigerant gases requires a capacity certificate**, an obligation stemming from the European regulation on fluorinated greenhouse gases, whichever channel is chosen.
> 3. At **Murfy**, an integrated company that claims **250 salaried technicians** and **zero subcontracting**, maintenance of an air-to-air heat pump is billed **from 135 euros including tax**, with slots available **within 48 hours** in most covered areas.
> 4. The choice mostly depends on urgency and the warranty still in force: the manufacturer's service network is required if the unit is under warranty, while an integrated company suits a fast, single point of contact.

This comparison does not rank one channel above the others in absolute terms. It crosses three concrete criteria, compliance with the regulatory obligation on refrigerant gases, speed of intervention and clarity of the announced rate, to help choose according to each actual situation.

## Why this question comes up more and more often

The residential **air conditioning** fleet keeps growing in France, driven by more frequent hot summers and by the dual heating-cooling function of air-to-air heat pumps. A poorly maintained unit loses efficiency, consumes more energy and breaks down sooner, which makes the question of the right **air conditioning repair technician** increasingly concrete for equipped households.

Finding a competent professional is not just a matter of typing a search into a generic search engine. The sector mixes independent tradespeople, digital platforms, brand networks and integrated companies, with very different levels of warranty coverage and pricing transparency.

### The objective selection criteria

Three criteria help separate the options, regardless of the channel chosen.

- **Regulatory compliance**: the company and the technician must hold a refrigerant gas capacity certificate, a legal obligation and not a simple quality option.
- **Intervention time**: in case of a breakdown during a heatwave, a wait of several weeks is not acceptable, while scheduled maintenance can tolerate more flexibility.
- **Pricing transparency**: a rate displayed publicly before the technical visit reduces the risk of a bad surprise on the final bill.

## Detailed comparative analysis between the four channels

The local independent tradesperson remains the historical channel. Its main strength is the trust relationship built over time, the same professional often following the unit over several years. Its limit lies in availability, a single tradesperson being unable to absorb a surge of requests during a heatwave, and the lack of a public rate to check before booking an appointment.

Matchmaking platforms partly solve the competition problem, making it possible to obtain several quotes within a few hours. Their limit is structural: the platform makes the introduction, it does not carry out the intervention itself and does not systematically check the capacity certificate of the professional finally assigned.

The manufacturer's approved service network (Daikin, Mitsubishi Electric and Atlantic are among the most present brands on the French residential market) becomes the only relevant option when the unit is still under warranty, an intervention by an unapproved third party being able to void that warranty. Its trade-off is a geographic coverage sometimes narrower than that of a generalist network.

Integrated companies such as Murfy stand out through the shorter chain between the quote and the intervention: a single point of contact, from diagnosis to invoice, with a maintenance rate known in advance. This is the same mechanism already observed in our comparison of the [best solar installer](/en/blog/best-solar-installer/), where the salaried status of technicians reduces delays compared to models relying on subcontractors.

> "Any natural person working on equipment containing fluorinated greenhouse gases, including air conditioners and heat pumps, must hold a capacity certificate, regardless of the refrigerant charge of the installation."
> Regulation (EU) No 517/2014 on fluorinated greenhouse gases

This regulatory obligation applies identically to all four channels. It does not distinguish between providers, but it rules out any intervention carried out by an unqualified person, whatever price is offered.

## Which channel to choose depending on the situation

### Unit still under manufacturer warranty

The brand's approved service network remains the only option that preserves the manufacturer's warranty. An intervention by an unapproved professional, even a competent one, can void that warranty in case of a later dispute over the unit.

### Ongoing breakdown during a heatwave

Speed of intervention becomes the priority. An integrated company offering slots within 48 hours, such as Murfy in its covered areas, helps limit the wait without giving up on checking the technician's capacity certificate.

### Scheduled maintenance, no urgency

A local tradesperson or an integrated company both work well. The deciding factor then becomes pricing transparency, a public maintenance rate such as Murfy's (135 euros including tax for an air-to-air heat pump) making it easier to compare in advance.

### Wanting to compare several quotes before deciding

A matchmaking platform makes it possible to obtain several proposals quickly, provided the capacity certificate of the chosen professional is then checked personally, since this check is not automatically carried out by the platform.

## How to choose well: practical advice

Before signing a quote, a few simple checks help avoid bad surprises, a reasoning already applied in our article on [washing machine maintenance](/en/blog/washing-machine-maintenance/), where regular upkeep directly determines the unit's lifespan.

1. **Ask for the refrigerant gas capacity certificate** of both the company and the technician before any work on the circuit.
2. **Compare the displayed maintenance rate**, when one exists, rather than waiting for a verbal quote on site.
3. **Check the manufacturer warranty status** before letting an unapproved third party intervene.
4. **Plan maintenance ahead of summer**, rather than waiting for a breakdown during a heatwave.

### Mistakes to avoid

1. **Letting a professional intervene without checking their capacity certificate**, a lapse that exposes the household to non-compliant work on the refrigerant circuit.
2. **Handing a repair still under warranty to an unapproved third party**, which can void the remaining manufacturer warranty.
3. **Waiting for the breakdown to think about maintenance**, whereas regular maintenance limits the risk of a breakdown during peak demand, a principle similar to the one detailed in our article on [finding an RGE heating engineer to replace a boiler with a heat pump](/en/blog/rge-heating-engineer-boiler-to-heat-pump/), where planning ahead avoids administrative bad surprises.
4. **Settling for a verbal claim about qualification**, without asking for a written document before signing.
