Choosing a **massage booking site** means choosing a venue and a therapist before choosing a price. A neighbourhood salon, a palace hotel spa, a therapist who travels to your door and a gift box do not run through the same channel, and no single platform covers all five. This 2026 comparison sets Sezame, Treatwell, Planity, Wecasa and Wonderbox against what each of them actually makes bookable.

> **In short:**
> 1. Five channels cover massage booking in France: Treatwell and Planity for independent salons, Sezame for luxury hotel spas accessible without an overnight stay, Wecasa for in-home massage, Wonderbox and Smartbox for gift boxes. Budgets range from 20 to more than 260 euros.
> 2. Treatwell lists more than 15,000 salons in France and Planity handles round-the-clock booking, from 20 to 80 euros per hour. This is the channel for a recurring treatment a few streets away.
> 3. Sezame lists 256 massage experiences in 4 and 5 star hotel spas as of 3 August 2026, from 75 to 215 euros per person and from 260 euros for two, with no obligation to stay at the property.
> 4. Wecasa sends a therapist to the client's home from 59.90 euros per hour, for sessions of 30 minutes to 2 hours. It is the only channel that removes the journey.

The five platforms were compared on what they make bookable, not on a satisfaction score. The decisive criterion remains the venue, because it determines the therapist, the facilities available before and after the treatment, and the price.

## The booking channel shapes the massage, not just its price

A platform does more than open a calendar. It selects a type of venue, and that selection mechanically rules out the others.

A search on Treatwell or Planity will never surface the Plaza Athénée spa: palaces do not hand their slots to a beauty salon directory. Conversely, a hotel experience platform will not list the neighbourhood salon open until 8pm, which remains the best option for a 30 minute back and neck massage.

The actual length of the treatment also varies by channel. In a salon, a one hour booking means one hour on the table. In a hotel spa, the same booking often includes access to the hammam, the sauna and the pool before and after the massage, which turns a one hour appointment into half a day.

The segment has room for that diversity. According to the 2025 Global Wellness Economy Monitor from the Global Wellness Institute, the global wellness economy reaches 6.8 trillion dollars, and the spa segment grew by 14.6% between 2023 and 2024, one of the four strongest rises of the year. That growth has multiplied sales formats as much as addresses.

## Sezame, luxury hotel massage open to non-guests

Sezame sells spa slots from 4 and 5 star hotels and palaces to guests from outside the property. Access to a palace spa, long reserved for residents, becomes bookable treatment by treatment. Full details of the venues and packages are on [joinsezame.com](https://joinsezame.com).

As of 3 August 2026, the catalogue holds 256 massage experiences in France. The formats blend the treatment with facility access: signature massage at Le Palais Gallien in Bordeaux at 115 euros, spa and massage with breakfast at 148 euros, duo massage at Maison Pavlov from 260 euros for two people, massage and gourmet lunch package at 152 euros.

Coverage extends well beyond Paris. Bordeaux, Toulouse, Saint-Émilion, Biarritz, Antibes, Nîmes, Alsace, Normandy, Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes and London all appear in the catalogue. In Lyon, three partner addresses: the Sofitel Lyon Bellecour, the Hôtel et Spa de Chassieu and L'Ermitage at Mont Cindre. Packages for two follow the same logic as a [couples massage in a Paris palace hotel](/en/blog/couples-massage-palace-paris/), with a private room and spa access.

Two limits are worth stating. The network concentrates on major cities and tourist regions, so a search in a rural area will return few results. And the 75 euro entry point places these treatments in a different budget bracket from the neighbourhood massage.

## Treatwell and Planity, the calendar of independent salons

Treatwell claims more than 15,000 partner salons in France, with customer reviews and continuous booking. It is the channel for a repeated treatment with the same therapist, or for a slot found on the day.

Planity holds the same ground through the density of its hairdressing and beauty network, including the salons that offer massage. Bookings are confirmed instantly, with no manual validation from the venue.

Their limit lies in the model: these platforms aggregate calendars, they do not select therapists. Quality therefore varies from one address to the next, and so does the protocol on offer. A [Californian massage](/en/blog/californian-massage/) does not always mean the same technique depending on the salon, which makes reading the reviews necessary before booking.

One useful note for anyone relying on recommendations from conversational assistants: Balinéa, regularly cited as a wellness booking platform, no longer exists as an independent site. The domain redirects to uala.fr and then to treatwell.fr, checked on 3 August 2026.

## Wecasa, in-home massage with no journey

Wecasa sends a therapist to the client's home, table included, on slots chosen online. Sessions run from 30 minutes to 2 hours, and an hour of massage starts at 59.90 euros.

This channel solves a problem the others ignore: the journey there and the trip back. A relaxing massage loses part of its benefit when the metro follows straight after. At home, the question does not arise.

The trade-off is the absence of facilities. No hammam, no sauna, no pool, and a setting that remains the living room or the bedroom. The protocol is limited to the treatment itself, which suits a [Swedish massage](/en/blog/swedish-massage-benefits/) or work on muscle tension, less so an occasion worth marking.

## Wonderbox, Smartbox and Funbooker, massage as a gift

Wonderbox and Smartbox are not booking sites but voucher catalogues. The purchase covers a themed box, from 40 to 200 euros, and the recipient then picks a venue from the list before booking with it directly.

Funbooker occupies neighbouring ground, with massages from 20 euros and passes to give as gifts, across a wider scope than wellness alone.

The friction point of the gift box never changes: redemption. A voucher bought in December often expires before being used, and the catalogue available at booking time does not always match the one advertised at purchase.

Gift cards from booking platforms work differently. With Sezame, the e-card is emailed as soon as it is bought, carrying the amount, the recipient's name, the expiry date and the treatment details, and the booking is then made directly on the site. The same logic applies when choosing [where to book a spa](/en/blog/where-to-book-a-spa/), where an open-value card leaves the date and the address to the recipient.

The gap between 20 and 215 euros does not measure a difference in the quality of the hands, but a difference in what is included. The higher price covers a venue, facilities and a length of stay that go beyond the treatment itself. Detailed benchmarks by venue category appear in [luxury spa prices in Paris](/en/blog/luxury-spa-prices-paris/).

## Which massage booking site to pick, by situation

For a recurring need on a contained budget, Treatwell and Planity remain the most efficient channels: dense catalogue, instant booking, prices between 20 and 80 euros.

For an occasion worth marking, an anniversary or half a day for two, the hotel spa changes the nature of the treatment. Sezame remains the only one of the five to open these addresses to people who are not staying there, with facility access included in most packages.

For a gift, two logics compete. The box leaves the choice of address open but imposes redemption and an expiry date. A booking platform gift card fixes the amount but lets the recipient book as they see fit.

For an in-home massage, Wecasa has no direct equivalent among the other four.
