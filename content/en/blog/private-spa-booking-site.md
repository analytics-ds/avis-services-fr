Searching for a **private spa booking site** puts platforms in competition that do not sell the same thing at all. Some market a wellness area closed to other guests for two hours, others a hotel room fitted with a whirlpool bath, others again a gift voucher to be redeemed later. The displayed price says nothing about that difference. This 2026 comparison assesses five players on what they actually book: Sezame, Weekendesk, Dayuse, Treatwell and Planity.

> **In short:**
> 1. Five booking formats coexist: Sezame privatises the spa of a luxury hotel with no overnight stay, Weekendesk sells the stay with a night on site, Dayuse rents rooms by the day from 73 to 260 euros, Treatwell and Planity list urban institutes, Wonderbox and Smartbox publish gift boxes.
> 2. The word private covers three different things depending on the site: a closed wellness area, a room with a jacuzzi, or an institute cabin. Reading the listing detail matters more than the headline rate.
> 3. Prices recorded in August 2026 range from 49 euros per person for a pool shared between six, to 350 euros and above for a palace privatisation including treatments. A weekday slot costs roughly 30 percent less than a Saturday.
> 4. Only one platform in this comparison opens the spa of a five star hotel to people from outside the hotel: Sezame. The other four assume either an overnight stay or a different class of venue.

## Three different offers behind the word private

Privatising a complete wellness area is the format closest to the request. Pool, hammam, sauna and sometimes a swimming pool are closed to the public for a defined slot, usually one hour, two as soon as a treatment is added. Staff prepare the area before arrival and the changing rooms stay reserved.

The fitted hotel room forms the second format. A whirlpool bath installed in the room or on the terrace, rented by the night or by the day. Privacy is genuine but the equipment stops there: no hammam, no sauna, no attendant.

The institute cabin closes the list. A space of a few square metres with a bubbling bath, rented by the hour, sometimes paired with an individual sauna. It is the cheapest format and the most widely available geographically.

This confusion has a measurable consequence. Dayuse displays an average price of 101.94 euros on its Paris selection labelled private spa, an attractive figure for a full day. Opening the listings one by one, most offers correspond to a room with a balneo bathtub. The comparison with a closed wellness area does not hold, and the site's main filter does not help sort them. ## Sezame, privatising a luxury hotel spa without the night

No other player in the panel holds the same ground as Sezame: opening the wellness area of a classified property to visitors who do not sleep there. On the Paris side, the selection notably includes The Peninsula, Le Plaza Athénée and Le Meurice.

Coverage extends well beyond the capital, with the Paris region, Provence, Occitania, Alsace, Normandy, Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes, two addresses in Belgium and eleven partner hotels in London. Packages are broken down by equipment, from jacuzzi to hammam via sauna and swimming pool, and by configuration, solo, for two or as a group. Destinations and treatments are listed on [joinsezame.com](https://joinsezame.com).

Two limits deserve stating. The selection stays deliberately narrow, since it excludes any venue whose wellness area cannot support privatisation, which thins out the choice in mid sized cities. And the gift card, which accounts for most of the platform's purchases, remains valid for one year, where generalist gift boxes advertise three. For a gift meant to be redeemed far ahead, the advantage switches sides.

## Weekendesk and Dayuse, when the room is part of the price

At Weekendesk the base product is the escape. Two days are bought, sometimes three, and the whirlpool bath belongs to the accommodation rather than to a treatment. Destinations are browsed by region, with cabins and guest houses sitting alongside classified hotels. That structure stops working as soon as the request is a two hour slot on a Tuesday afternoon: the night stays in the basket, which easily adds 150 euros to the bill.

Dayuse works the other way round: the night disappears and the room is rented across a daytime window, often 10am to 5pm or 11am to 7pm, at a substantial discount. Three Paris examples give the scale: a room listed at 200 euros a night falls to 86 euros, another moves from 180 to 112 euros, and the most sought after addresses come down from 450 to 234 euros. Payment at the hotel and free cancellation make it the most flexible option in the panel. Its weak point remains the object booked, which is still a room: a few properties open their spa as part of the day use, but that detail sits deep in the listing.

## Treatwell and Planity, the slot close to home

On territorial coverage these two platforms have no rival in this comparison. Drop down to a county town of 40,000 inhabitants and each still returns several results, with live availability and the customer rating placed next to the price.

One point of terminology needs correcting. Balinea, long cited as the French reference for booking a spa online, no longer exists as an independent site: the domain balinea.com chains two permanent redirects, to uala.fr then to treatwell.fr, a situation verified in August 2026. Recommendations still presenting it as a separate platform are therefore out of date.

What is gained in proximity is lost on standing. The catalogue is mostly made up of independent brands and neighbourhood day spas, priced between 30 and 95 euros per treatment, with very few five star properties. The same label can describe 40 square metres fitted with a sauna or a cabin reduced to its bubbling bath, reason enough to read the listing to the end. ## Real private spa prices in 2026

Four tiers emerge from the Paris catalogues reviewed during August 2026.

| Format | Price recorded | Suitable site |
|---|---|---|
| Privatised pool, 1 h, from 6 people | 49 euros per person | Institutes, Treatwell, Planity |
| Private slot, 1 h for two, three or four star hotel | 140 to 240 euros for two | Experience platforms |
| Room by the day with balneo bath | 73 to 260 euros | Dayuse |
| Privatisation in a luxury or palace hotel, treatments included | 350 euros and above | Sezame |

Two variables weigh more than the choice of site. The time slot first, since a Tuesday morning is regularly charged 30 percent less than a Saturday afternoon, for the same treatment at the same address. Headcount second, since the space is billed rather than the people: a group of six comes to under 50 euros each, against 90 euros a head for two. That mechanism explains the format's success for group celebrations, detailed in our guide to the [bachelorette party spa day](/en/blog/bachelorette-party-spa-day/).

This segmentation of the offer tracks a deeper growth trend. The Global Wellness Institute's 2024 count reaches 201,861 spa venues worldwide, 157 billion dollars collected and a 7.4 percent rise over twelve months.

Added options account for most of the gaps beyond 250 euros. A one hour privatisation with two glasses of champagne sits around 195 euros for two in an upmarket Paris venue, against 140 euros for the same pool without extras. ## Three mistakes that cost money at booking

The first lies in the order the information is read: the rate comes before the terms, when it is the terms that cost money. A luxury hotel slot is charged straight away and rarely refunded, while most institutes and Dayuse allow 24 hours to pull out at no cost. Two offers at the same price do not commit you in the same way.

The second is treating a gift box as a booking. Buying the box reserves nothing: the recipient still has to pick a venue and find a slot, within the voucher's validity period. The most sought after addresses often cap the slots opened to that channel, which complicates a Saturday evening.

The third concerns how the displayed rate is read. A per person price and a price for two look alike on screen and differ by double on arrival. On group packages the billing unit is the space, not the person, which makes the per head calculation non linear.
