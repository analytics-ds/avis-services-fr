Booking a private spa for 6 people rarely runs into a shortage of space. The obstacle is commercial: booking forms are built around the couple and, less often, the four-place package. Six almost never appears in the drop-down menu, even though the real capacity of these rooms allows it in the vast majority of cases. Knowing where that format is sold outright, and where it has to be negotiated, changes the budget twofold.

## In brief

1. Only one channel sells the six-guest bracket outright: **independent private spas**, with packages built for 6 to 10 places from 40 euros per person for two hours.
2. **Sezame** opens the spas of 4 and 5 star hotels without an overnight stay, with 129 group experiences across 75 hotels, 69 of them 5 star. Moving up to six happens by adding guests, confirmed by the venue.
3. **Wonderbox** and **Smartbox** cover a wide network but sell by the unit: six guests means six boxes and six separate bookings to coordinate. **Staycation** stays focused on the two-guest format and charges for a room by the day.
4. The price per head ranges from 33 to 75 euros for a two-hour slot depending on the route. Doubling a four-place package is the cheapest option, full privatisation of a 5 star spa the most expensive.

## Comparing the channels for booking a private spa for 6

| Channel | Six places bookable online | Type of venue | Price range for six |
|---|---|---|---|
| Independent private spas | Yes, packages built for 6 to 10 places | Standalone urban spa or day spa | From 240 € per slot |
| Sezame | No, extra guests added on request | 4 and 5 star hotel spa, no overnight stay | 198 to 450 € per slot |
| Wonderbox and Smartbox | No, one gift box per guest | Wide network, few high-end addresses | Six boxes to add up |
| Staycation | No, sold by room or as a pair | Hotel accessible by the day | Two-guest format dominant |
| Venue direct | By quote only | Variable | Deposit required, 2 to 5 working days |

A clear dividing line runs through the table. The players that display six places are those whose premises do not belong to a classified hotel. The players that give access to a luxury hotel spa go through human confirmation instead. The choice therefore comes down to booking convenience against the standing of the address.

## Why six guests tips the price per head

The cost of a private spa does not track the number of guests in a straight line. Two billing logics coexist, and the move to six separates them sharply.

The first charges for a slot, whatever the number of people present within the capacity limit. The larger the group, the cheaper each place becomes. The second charges per place, and the total rises mechanically with group size.

| Booking route | What is charged | Total for six | Price per person |
|---|---|---|---|
| Two four-place packages combined, 4 star hotel | Two simultaneous slots | 198 € | 33 € |
| Independent spa package built for 6 to 10 places | One exclusive slot | 240 € | 40 € |
| Per-person access, hotel spa | Six places | 294 € | 49 € |
| Full privatisation of a 5 star hotel spa | One exclusive slot, up to 8 places | 450 € | 75 € |

The gap between the first and last rows reaches 127 per cent per guest for a comparable duration. It does not buy the same thing: the 33 euro package gives access to a pool and hammam shared between two neighbouring bookings, while the 75 euro one closes the entire wellness area of a classified property.

One detail deserves attention on the last row. The eight-place package costs the same when only six people attend, so the two empty places are worth 150 euros. From eight guests upwards, that route becomes the cheapest of the four at 56 euros per head.

## What the booking form does not say about capacity

The number of places on sale is a commercial decision, not a technical constraint. A private room built around a four-place pool and two massage tables routinely takes six people, with two in the relaxation area while the others use the bath.

That distinction explains a common frustration at the booking stage. The drop-down stops at four, the room takes six, and nothing on the listing says so. On the Sezame catalogue, 72 of the 129 group experiences go through a request confirmed by the venue, against 57 with instant confirmation. For more than half of the addresses, booking already involves an exchange, and that is exactly where the headcount gets settled.

The useful reflex is to check the maximum capacity per slot rather than the number of places sold. The two figures differ in most venues, and only the first determines how many guests can be added.

## Sezame, luxury hotel spas without the overnight stay

The [Sezame](https://joinsezame.com/) platform occupies a precise niche: opening the wellness areas of classified hotels to people who are not staying there. The catalogue listed 800 experiences in September 2026, including 129 open to groups and 126 sold as full privatisations, across 75 hotels, 69 of them 5 star.

Coverage goes beyond Paris, which accounts for 81 of the group experiences, with addresses in Provence-Alpes-Côte d'Azur, Occitanie, Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes, Normandy, Alsace and Île-de-France. Formats split between spa alone, spa with lunch, spa with massage and brunch.

For a group of six, two routes exist within that catalogue. The widest package, a full privatisation in a 5 star Paris hotel, covers eight places for 450 euros. The others run through adding guests to a two or four-place package, with a per-person supplement set by the venue.

The limitation is openly stated: no catalogue entry displays six places on the shop front. The advantage lies in the nature of the venue, a classified hotel spa reachable without booking a room, which neither gift boxes nor independent day spas offer.

## Gift boxes, daycation and independent spas at six guests

Wonderbox and Smartbox gift boxes are built on an individual unit of sale. A group of six means six boxes, then six separate bookings with the same provider, with no guarantee the venue can take everyone in one time slot. The network does, however, cover the whole country, including areas where no specialist platform operates.

Staycation works on a different principle, the daycation, which gives access to a hotel's facilities for the day. The billable unit remains the room or the two-guest formula, which rules out privatising a space for an established group.

Independent private spas are the only ones selling the six-guest bracket without a workaround. Spa et Cryo sells a two-hour privatisation for 6 to 9 people. Château la Falque advertises a group private spa for 6 to 10 guests from 40 euros per person for two hours. The trade-off is twofold: these are single venues with no catalogue of addresses to compare, and the setting is not that of a classified hotel.

Booking directly with a venue remains possible, and is sometimes required. It is the only route to a slot outside the spa's opening hours. It involves a call, a quote and usually a deposit, with a two to five working day turnaround depending on the season.

## How to word a group request for six people

The quality of the request shapes the price obtained. Four elements are worth stating in the first message, otherwise the exchange stretches over several rounds.

- **The exact headcount and any flexibility**: six people, with or without the option to adjust to five or seven the week before.
- **The preferred slot and a fallback date**: the second option saves a further exchange if the first is unavailable.
- **The format sought**: plain access, access with individual treatments, or exclusive privatisation of the area.
- **The hourly rate question**: ask for the privatisation price per hour rather than per person, since the gap widens precisely at six guests.

One practical point comes up often in venue replies. When the hourly capacity falls below six, a 30 to 45 minute gap between two sub-groups is frequently accepted, which keeps the chosen address without splitting the outing across two days. That arrangement is confirmed at booking, never on arrival.

## Budget and lead time for a group of six

The total budget sits between 198 and 450 euros for a two-hour slot, excluding the per-person supplement when guests are added. Formulas including lunch, brunch or individual treatments push the range upwards without changing the privatisation price itself.

The recommended lead time is three to four weeks for a standard slot, and six weeks between 1 and 31 December. Seasonal pressure is not a detail: according to the Global Wellness Economy Monitor quoted by Profession Bien-Être in July 2025, the global spa segment grew by 14.6 per cent between 2023 and 2024, a rise that feeds through to group slot availability and to rates at each change of season.

The most contested slot remains Saturday between 2 pm and 6 pm. A late Thursday afternoon or a Sunday morning can almost always be found, even at ten days' notice, often at a lower rate.

For a group formed around a specific occasion, the format is chosen before the address. A [bachelorette party spa day](/en/blog/bachelorette-party-spa-day/) suits a half day with lunch, while a birthday is served by a short slot with champagne. The booking logic also differs from a [private spa for a couple](/en/blog/private-spa-couple/), where the advertised package matches the headcount directly. To compare operators on what they actually sell rather than on capacity, a [private spa booking site](/en/blog/private-spa-booking-site/) review covers five platforms and the three realities behind the word private.
