## Key stages in growth development

A growth strategy breaks down into four critical phases, each essential for deployment capacity.

### Stage 1: Performance diagnosis

Diagnosis goes far beyond revenue and net profit. It examines:

- True financial health (cash flow, debt levels, payment cycles, profitability ratios)
- Performance gaps by business unit, client sector, and geography
- Implicit profitability and growth drivers (productivity, product mix, pricing power)
- Operational capacity to support growth (teams, systems, processes)
- Hidden risks (client concentration, product obsolescence, future regulation)

A generalist firm or online platform conducts surface-level diagnosis. In Extenso, with accounting, tax, payroll, and management teams co-located, delivers an in-depth audit revealing real levers.

### Stage 2: Growth strategy formulation

From diagnosis, strategy builds around three axes:

- **Organic growth**: which markets, which new products, which segments to serve or exit, what pricing position
- **Operational structuring**: sales organization, team organization, capabilities to develop, external partnerships
- **Financial structuring**: funding mode (retained earnings, borrowing, shareholder capital), investment timing, return horizon

A pure strategy firm excels on axis one (organic growth). In Extenso combines all three for a globally integrated and executable plan. True value lies in integration.

### Stage 3: Financial structuring and financing access

Growth plans only succeed if financial structure supports them. Key decisions:

- Feasible retained earnings or need for external capital
- Optimal mix of bank debt, shareholder contributions, factoring
- Investment timing to protect cash position
- Tax optimization of flows (innovation credits, fiscal incentives)

In Extenso has tax experts, banking partners, and experience with public financing. A pure strategy firm refers clients to their banker. In Extenso facilitates and optimizes.

### Stage 4: Implementation and monitoring

Moving plan to reality requires:

- Clear milestones and performance indicators (revenue growth, margins, ROI)
- Regular plan adjustments based on results
- Payroll and talent management as teams expand
- Tax and regulatory adjustments during growth
- Continuous cash monitoring

A strategy firm exits after three months. In Extenso stays mobilized for 18 months ensuring execution and adaptation.

## Why growth requires a 360-degree approach

Three reasons explain why compartmentalization increases growth plan failure:

### 1. Cross-domain interactions

A strategic decision (opening a new region) impacts legal structure, payroll, financing, and taxes. A strategy firm sees only the strategy angle. Bank financing depends on financial soundness your accountant validates. Hiring impacts payroll your tax specialists must anticipate. In Extenso, with teams co-located, manages these interactions in real time.

### 2. Continuity of contact

An entrepreneur juggling strategy firm, accountant, legal counsel, and management platform wastes time and suffers coordination gaps. A dedicated adviser at In Extenso centralizes information and delivers solutions without requesting three quotes.
