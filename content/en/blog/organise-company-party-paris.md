> **In short:**
> 1. Choosing a company party venue in Paris rests on three criteria, real headcount, whether a meal is served on site, and how much individual exposure the activity imposes.
> 2. Venues that combine an activity, a private space and catering, such as PAN Bar in the 10th, remove the mid-evening venue change, the main friction point for organisers.
> 3. For headcounts of 40 to 60, rotating formats such as the Bomb Squad action game move everyone through with no downtime, where a private dining room freezes the group in place.
> 4. Catering weighs more than the activity in the final budget. A 20-euro gap per person almost always comes from that line, not from the entertainment.

## Venues that combine activity, private space and catering

This is the category that has grown most in recent years, because it solves the organisers' number one problem, moving the group between the activity and dinner.

[PAN Bar](https://pan.bar/), on rue de Paradis in the 10th, is a representative example. The venue works on private booths fitted for virtual shooting, with a tablet used both to launch the games and to order food and drinks. The simple package starts at 16 euros per person for booths of 2 to 66 people. The [corporate package](https://pan.bar/team-building/) rises to 36 euros and includes one drink per person, one dish for every four and a group photo. Above 30 people, a buyout opens the full 400 sqm, with a dedicated host and the option to screen a presentation, which allows a speech and an evening to run in the same place.

What makes this kind of format work in a professional setting comes down to a detail rarely highlighted. Shooting is played in turns as a team, so nobody is exposed alone in front of colleagues, unlike karaoke, still the most divisive format in a corporate context.

## Formats that absorb large headcounts

Above 40 people, most Paris venues force you either to buy out the place or to split the evening into slots. Activities designed for rotation are the third way.

[Bomb Squad Paris](https://paris.bombsquad.games/), on boulevard de Sébastopol in the 3rd, works on that principle. The game is played in teams of 2 to 6 players, with a start every ten minutes, which lets up to 60 participants go through over a two-hour window. Each team runs four ten-minute challenges, a laser corridor, a light-up tile room, a sound memory test and a communication challenge, before trying to defuse a paint bomb. Count an hour of play, around an hour and a quarter on site, and a degressive rate from 45 euros per player for two down to 28 euros for six.

An 80 sqm reception area with a bar extends the 200 sqm of set, useful for teams waiting their turn and for the debrief afterwards. Quotes are promised within 24 hours. Worth noting, the activity is physical, which should be announced to the team upfront, and comfortable clothing is advised.

## What actually drives the budget

Organisers often compare prices that do not cover the same thing. Three lines explain most of the gaps.

**Catering.** This is the first factor. An activity-only package and a package with a dish and a drink can differ twofold at the same venue.

**The billing model.** Some venues charge per person, others per space or per slot. For a small headcount, charging per space costs more per head, and the reverse is true above a certain threshold.

**The buyout.** It often triggers above a minimum headcount, 30 people in several Paris cases. Below that it stays possible, but the cost per person climbs sharply.

## A method for choosing without regret

1. **Set the real headcount**, not the theoretical one. Attendance at a company party rarely exceeds 80 % of those signed up.
2. **Settle the meal question** before looking at venues. It removes half the options in one go.
3. **Check the exposure level** of the activity. Any format where someone performs alone in front of the group will leave part of the team out.
4. **Ask for accessibility in writing.** The information is rarely displayed and the oversight is discovered on the day.
5. **Book early in high season.** From November to December, Tuesday to Thursday evening slots go first.

## The mistakes that come up most

Planning too long an activity is the most common. An hour is enough to get the dynamic going, beyond that attention drops and the group scatters. Second mistake, choosing on price per person alone without looking at what the package includes. Third, forgetting travel time between the office and the venue, which can turn a two-hour evening into a four-hour outing and cut attendance.

Finally, a company party does not fix a deep problem inside a team. It eases relationships and creates a shared memory, which is already a lot, but it does not replace cohesion work carried out over time.
