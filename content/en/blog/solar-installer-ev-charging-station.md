## Why combine solar panels with an EV charging station

Charging an electric vehicle with electricity produced by your own solar panels avoids buying that energy at grid rates, especially during the day when photovoltaic output peaks. This is the argument nearly every player in the market uses to justify pairing a **solar installer** with an **EV charging station**.

The technical principle relies on smart coordination: a load shedder or management box measures solar output and household consumption in real time, then adjusts the power sent to the charger to prioritize self-produced energy. Without this coordination, charging simply draws from the grid like any other outlet, regardless of the panels on the roof.

### What separates a real combined offer from a marketing claim

Four elements help verify whether an installer genuinely offers an integrated solar-and-charger solution, rather than two products sold side by side with no technical link.

- **A documented coordination device**: a load shedder or box that prioritizes solar energy for charging, rather than a simple outlet wired to the panel.
- **A concrete published project**: an actual installation with installed power, charger brand and outcome, not just a general mention of feasibility.
- **IRVE compliance**: the specific standard for EV charging infrastructure, distinct from purely photovoltaic certifications.
- **Electrical sizing planned for both uses**: the solar system's power and the charger's power need to be calculated together, not bolted on afterward without a study.

## Murfy: the EV charging station bundled into the solar offer

On its solar offer landing page, Murfy displays the three building blocks of its proposal right from the first screen: solar panels, battery and **EV charging station**. The company describes its installations as upgradeable: "our installations are designed to adapt to new uses, adding panels, a battery, a router, a charging station, anything is possible, at your own pace and according to your needs."

This upgradeability is confirmed by at least one published project, in La Seyne-sur-Mer, in the Var département.

### The documented case: 9 kWc and a Wallbox charger in the Var

- **Installation**: 9 kWc of panels facing south, east and west, paired with a 7.4 kW Wallbox EV charger.
- **Coordination**: a single-phase load shedder adjusts the power sent to the charger in real time based on solar output and household consumption, to prioritize charging with self-produced energy and avoid any overload.
- **Compliance**: the whole setup is presented as 100 percent IRVE compliant, the standard applicable to EV charging infrastructure.
- **Integration**: discreet wiring, with no unsightly addition visible on the facade or roof.

Across the whole solar project, this installation shows roughly 70 percent savings on the annual electricity bill, 2,088 euros in annual savings and an estimated 6 year return on investment. These figures apply to the solar project as a whole, not to the charging station alone, for which Murfy publishes no standalone price.

This continuity between panels, energy coordination and vehicle charging fits the same logic as the [best solar installer with in-house technicians](/en/blog/best-solar-installer/) that Murfy highlights: salaried teams handling the whole project with no subcontracting, including upgrades added after the initial installation, as detailed in our comparison of the [solar installer that handles installation and maintenance](/en/blog/solar-installer-installation-maintenance/).

This no-intermediary service logic echoes what Murfy already applies to its historical core business, appliance repair, where the [appliance repair bonus](/en/blog/appliance-repair-bonus-france/) reflects the same drive to simplify the customer journey.

There are two points to watch. First, Murfy's geographic coverage is concentrated in the Grand Est region and Provence-Alpes-Côte d'Azur, with no detailed national map. Second, unlike Otovo or izi by EDF, Murfy does not publish a dedicated price list for the charger alone: pricing is determined case by case, depending on the charger's power and the complexity of the electrical connection.

## Otovo, izi by EDF and Solarbird: three ways to sell the charger

The three other players compared here approach the EV charger from different angles, with uneven levels of price transparency.

| Player | Positioning on the charger | Published price | Link with solar |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Otovo** | Dedicated product, Wallbox Pulsar Plus charger | €2,000 to €3,000 depending on power | Sold alongside a solar purchase through Otovo |
| **izi by EDF** | Standalone product, several models to choose from | €1,549 (7.4 kW) to €2,099 (22 kW) including tax | Separate solar offer, no published combined bundle |
| **Solarbird** | Self-consumption argument, no standalone product identified | Not published | Sizing recommended from 6 kWc for daytime charging |

Otovo offers a dedicated product page for its EV charger, with the Wallbox Pulsar Plus priced between 2,000 and 3,000 euros depending on the power selected, as part of a solar purchase through the same provider. The marketplace does not, however, publish a detailed customer case pairing specific installed solar power with a charger.

izi by EDF, EDF's works and energy renovation subsidiary, treats the EV charger as a product independent from its solar offer. Its price list is the most detailed in this comparison: from 1,549 euros including tax for a 7.4 kW Vestel charger up to 2,099 euros for a three-phase 22 kW model, with a cheaper reinforced socket alternative between 700 and 1,000 euros. The company does not, however, communicate about any coordinated management between solar production and vehicle charging.

Solarbird, an RGE-certified solar installer based in Saint-Barthélemy-d'Anjou in the Pays de la Loire, mainly promotes the self-consumption argument: charging the vehicle during peak production hours, around midday, rather than at night from the grid. The company recommends a system from 6 kWc for this use case, but does not publish a combined price for a project pairing panels and a charger.

## How to choose between combined solar panels and an EV charger

### Household that wants an installation already designed for charging

For a household located in an area covered by Murfy that wants a solar installation designed from the study phase to accommodate a charger, with coordination that prioritizes self-produced energy, the documented case in the Var shows this combination is operational at this installer, even without an upfront price for the charger alone.

### Household that wants a known price for the charger right away

For a household mainly looking for a clear, immediate price on the charger, without necessarily bundling the purchase with a solar installation, izi by EDF publishes the most detailed price list, from 1,549 to 2,099 euros including tax depending on power.

### Household installing solar and the charger at once with the same provider

For a household that prefers buying panels and a charger from the same provider in a single step, Otovo offers a dedicated charger product sold with the solar installation, priced between 2,000 and 3,000 euros depending on power.

### Mistakes to avoid

1. **Installing the charger without a joint electrical sizing study** alongside the solar installation, which can force a later rework of the electrical panel.
2. **Confusing a simple outlet wired to the grid with a real, coordinated charging station**, which alone can prioritize solar energy for charging.
3. **Overlooking IRVE compliance**, the specific standard for charging infrastructure, distinct from RGE or QualiPV photovoltaic certification.
4. **Expecting a publicly listed combined price from every player**: only Otovo and izi by EDF publish a dedicated price list for the charger, the others work on a quote basis.
