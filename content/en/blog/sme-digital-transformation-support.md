## Why digital transformation is becoming urgent for SMEs

Three external developments have accelerated the timeline.

**Mandatory electronic invoicing** (January 2026 deadline for standard-regime SMEs, then 2027 for micro-enterprises) forced every SME to revisit its supplier-to-accounting workflow. Previously optional, it is now a legal requirement. And as soon as invoice format changes, the question expands: if we're digitizing supplier invoices, why not payroll, contracts, quotations? Electronic invoicing pulled the thread.

**The cost of avoiding digitization is real**. A 2025 BPI France survey found that non-digitized SMEs lose 8 to 12 hours per week to manual data entry, file searches, and duplicate processing. For a 30-person SME, that is 240 to 360 hours annually, equivalent to 3 to 4 full-time employees devoted to data entry. By today's standards, this is unsustainable.

**Tools are maturing and becoming affordable**. Between 2018 and 2025, accounting software vendors (Ciel, EBP, Sage, etc.) reduced license costs by 40 percent, improved user interfaces, and standardized APIs. The learning curve dropped. Implementation firms know how to deploy them quickly. This was not true in 2015.

The moment is now. For an SME without IT expertise, expert guidance significantly reduces the risk of a failed launch.

## Digitization: where to start?

Project sequencing matters. A poor start can derail months of work.

**Stage 1: supplier documents (incoming invoices)**. Most urgent since January 2026, and gains are immediate. An SME receiving 100 invoices monthly saves 30 hours monthly by eliminating manual data entry alone. Process: set up centralized electronic filing (Docuware, Universign, or a well-structured shared folder), define approval workflow (who approves before accounting entry?), and integrate with accounting (either automatic via API or semi-automatic via .txt/CSV).

**Stage 2: customer invoicing (sales invoicing)**. Downstream. Auto-generation via invoicing software (rather than Excel), electronic signature if legally required, archival. Benefit: no re-entry to accounting, traceability, legal compliance.

**Stage 3: payroll and digital pay stub**. Employee online payslip access, stub signing, historical view. Reduces payroll-to-accounting re-entry because the software handles it automatically. For SMEs seeking deeper guidance on this area, consult the guide on [payroll outsourcing and HR administration](/en/blog/payroll-outsourcing-hr-admin/).

**Stage 4: shared documents** (contracts, amendments, expense reports, advances). Less urgent but logically linked after the first three.

A firm promising "everything at once, in one month" is unrealistic. In Extenso deploys a phased plan over 4 to 8 months.

## Costs and timeline for a typical SME transformation

Real example from 2026. 35-person SME, paper supplier invoices, Excel payroll, accounting software with no integrated workflow.

- **Initial audit**: 1 day, free, 10-point diagnostic to kickstart
- **Tool selection and licensing**: 1 month, accounting + payroll + digitization software, budget 6,000 €
- **Technical implementation**: 2 months, local reseller integrates tools with firm guidance
- **Team training**: 4 sessions, 8 hours each, spread over 3 months
- **Go-live**: end of month 3, production switch
- **Follow-up**: 3 months of support calls included, total timeline 6 months

**Total cost**: audit + consulting + local vendor fees, range 14,000 to 20,000 € before tax depending on office. SME internal cost: 1,800 to 2,500 € before tax monthly during project (estimated 60 internal project hours).

For comparison, a SaaS platform like Gestialink charges 5,000 € for selection and onboarding (no on-site training), then 200 to 400 €/month in subscription (12 months = 2,400 to 4,800 €). Saves 8,000 € on the project, higher risk of poor launch. This transformation often fits into a broader [growth strategy consulting](/en/blog/growth-strategy-consulting/) effort to ensure tools actually enable your company to scale.
