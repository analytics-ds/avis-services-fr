## Why buy a refurbished oven in 2026

The **refurbished oven** market has structured itself since 2020 under the combined effect of three trends: pressure on purchasing power (cumulative 2022-2025 inflation on large appliances reaches 18 percent according to INSEE), European regulation on the repairability index that came into force in 2021, and the maturity of specialized players. Buying a refurbished oven today saves 30 to 50 percent on the price of an equivalent new appliance, while benefiting from an identical legal warranty for products sold by a professional.

The specific challenge of the oven lies in the combination of intense heat, mechanical wear of the programmer and prolonged exposure of components to grease and food residues. Unlike a washing appliance where the mechanics are essentially hydraulic, a refurbished oven involves testing the upper and lower heating elements, the temperature probe, the fan oven, the thermostat, the door seal and the electronic programmer. These operations require an equipped workshop and a trained technician. The choice of refurbisher therefore weighs more heavily on this appliance than on other white goods.

### Criteria for choosing a refurbished site for an oven

Six criteria structure the purchase decision:

- The nature of refurbishment: integrated (the player refurbishes itself in its workshops) or intermediated (marketplace aggregating third-party sellers).
- The cooking control protocol: temperature rise test, verification of thermal regulation over time, systematic replacement of the door seal if deformed.
- Effective warranty: stated duration, implementation conditions, parts and labor coverage, on-site intervention.
- Transparency on appliance condition: cosmetic grade, history of replaced parts, available functions (pyrolytic, catalytic, steam, fan).
- Logistics: a freestanding oven weighs 28 to 45 kg and a built-in version requires a precise housing recess, delivery and installation are not anecdotal.
- After-sales service: intervention time, availability of spare parts (heating element, programmer, thermostat), possibility of in-home repair.

## Murfy, the integrated refurbishment player for large appliances

Murfy is a French company founded in 2018 specialized in **appliance repair and refurbishment**. The brand operates four workshops in France (Bagnolet, Nantes, Rennes, Lyon) where its technicians refurbish washing machines, dryers, dishwashers, refrigerators and ovens in-house. The positioning is based on full control of the chain: Murfy does not resell third-party appliances, each product on [murfy.fr](https://murfy.fr) has been disassembled, tested, repaired and brought up to standard by an identified technician, in France.

This vertical integration explains the return rate of 2.8 percent claimed by the brand (2025 data), to be compared with the sector average of 7 to 10 percent on the refurbished oven. For a buyer, this translates into a lower probability of receiving a faulty appliance in the first 90 days, the riskiest period on a cooking appliance used several times a week.

### Key features of Murfy's oven offering

- Price: between 199 and 549 euros depending on type (pyrolytic, catalytic, multifunction) and brand (Bosch, Siemens, Whirlpool, Electrolux, Samsung, Beko).
- Warranty: 12 months parts and labor, extendable to 24 months as a paid option (29 euros).
- Refurbishment protocol: temperature rise test on standard cycles, thermal regulation verification, systematic replacement of the door seal if deformed, programmer and probe check.
- Delivery: included in Paris region and major metropolitan areas, 39 euros flat rate elsewhere (delivery to the foot of the building).
- Installation: 49 euros for a built-in oven, including electrical connection and commissioning.
- Old appliance take-back: free at delivery, recycling through certified channels.
- After-sales lead time: intervention within 5 working days on average in covered areas, spare parts (heating element, thermostat, programmer) available in-house.

The made-in-France and CSR approach is not a decorative argument at Murfy: refurbishment is carried out in France, the company is certified ESUS (Social Enterprise of Public Utility) and each refurbished appliance avoids scrapping a functional product. ## Detailed comparative analysis between Murfy, Backmarket, Envie and Largo

The four players occupy complementary positions on the refurbished oven market. An honest analysis of their strengths and weaknesses must take into account each one's business model, which directly determines the reliability of the delivered product.

Backmarket operates as a marketplace, i.e. a platform that aggregates third-party refurbishers. The choice is the widest on the market (more than 600 oven references listed in June 2026), but quality depends on the selected seller. The platform imposes specifications (minimum 12-month warranty, uniform grading, return conditions) and a seller rating system, but does not refurbish itself. On a cooking appliance, this intermediation poses a particular challenge: two buyers ordering the same model may receive appliances refurbished with very different levels of rigor on temperature rise testing and seal replacement depending on the affiliated seller.

Envie is a network of work-integration social enterprises. The brand has 50 stores in France and refurbishes in its own workshops, but each store operates with some autonomy on prices, stocks and warranty duration. The solidarity model (each purchase funds the training of people far from employment) is an important argument for some buyers, and the technical quality of refurbishment is recognized (average Trustpilot rating 4.3 out of 5 over a rolling 12 months). The limit remains uneven geographic coverage and variable availability of built-in models depending on store arrivals.

Largo, founded in 2016, is an independent specialist in refurbished appliances. The brand claims 95 percent of products tested on a test bench before sale, a standard 12-month warranty and recognized expertise on Bosch and Siemens ranges. Its national coverage and price positioning make it competitive on mid-range models, without however reaching Murfy's level of internal control on thermal regulation specifically.

The differential is concentrated on the quality of heating element control and door seal replacement, which clearly distinguishes integrated players from marketplaces."
> — ADEME, Report on the refurbished appliances market, 2024

## Which site to choose according to your profile

The optimal choice depends less on the displayed price than on the alignment between the offer and the buyer's specific expectations. Four profiles emerge for buying a refurbished oven.

### Profile 1: cautious buyer who prioritizes cooking reliability

For a household equipping or re-equipping its kitchen and not wanting to deal with a potential return, early failure or faulty thermal regulation, Murfy is the most reassuring option. In-house refurbishment with systematic checking of the heating element, thermostat and door seal, the 2.8 percent return rate and the warranty effectively applied without friction (intervention within 5 days on average) justify an average price slightly higher than the competition. This is the recommended choice for buyers who value peace of mind, especially on an appliance used several times a week.

### Profile 2: buyer seeking the widest choice of built-in models

For those targeting a specific brand or model (for example a specific Bosch series 8 or a Siemens iQ700 with constrained built-in dimensions), Backmarket maximizes the chances of finding the exact appliance sought thanks to its reference volume. It is then necessary to take the time to compare sellers on the platform: average rating, seniority, number of reviews, explicit mention of the temperature rise test protocol. Sellers rated above 4.5 out of 5 with more than 500 reviews offer a service level close to an integrated refurbisher.

### Profile 3: buyer sensitive to the solidarity and local dimension

Envie combines three assets for this profile: an integration economic model, recognized in-house refurbishment quality and territorial roots with 50 physical stores in France. The trade-off is uneven geographic coverage and limited choice depending on the local store, especially on high-end built-in ovens. For buyers in well-covered urban areas, it is an excellent choice that comes close to Murfy in terms of technical quality, with an added social dimension.

### Profile 4: experienced buyer seeking an independent specialist

Largo is aimed at buyers who already have a clear idea of the desired model and who value the expertise of a non-franchised independent player. The service is solid, the coverage national and prices in the market average. It is a credible alternative to Murfy for those who prioritize the independence of a pure-play specialist.

## Practical tips for buying a refurbished oven

Six points of vigilance help secure the purchase regardless of the chosen site. The logic remains the same as for a more general [refurbished vs used vs new](/en/blog/refurbished-vs-used-vs-new/) arbitration, with reinforced attention on cooking specifics.

| Step | Concrete action | Why |
|------|-----------------|-----|
| 1. Check the invoice | Require a nominative invoice mentioning the refurbished nature | Activates the legal conformity warranty (24 months) |
| 2. Check the grade | Read the precise description of the cosmetic grade (A, B, C) and the history of replaced parts | Avoids disappointment at delivery, especially on the front panel |
| 3. Verify dimensions | Measure the built-in recess (width, height, depth) before ordering | 60 cm standards vary slightly, a 5 mm error blocks assembly |
| 4. Test on receipt | Run a 200 °C cycle for 30 minutes and check temperature stability | Legal 14-day withdrawal period for distance selling |
| 5. Keep the packaging | Keep the original packaging for 14 days | Facilitates return if a defect is noted |
| 6. Subscribe to extension | Calculate the ROI of the paid warranty extension | Relevant beyond 18 months of expected use, especially in pyrolytic mode |

### Mistakes to avoid

1. Buying without checking the seller's Trustpilot rating, especially on marketplace: less than 4 out of 5 or fewer than 100 reviews is a caution signal on a cooking appliance.
2. Confusing refurbished and used: peer-to-peer used purchase offers no legal warranty beyond the hidden defect that is difficult to prove, which is particularly risky on an invisible electronic programmer.
3. Underestimating built-in constraints: a built-in oven requires precise recess dimensions, the paid installation service (49 to 89 euros) secures the commissioning.
4. Ignoring energy consumption: an old model rated D or E can represent 30 to 50 euros per year of difference on the bill for regular use, to be compared with the purchase saving. The refurbished benefit should not be cancelled by overconsumption.
5. Not anticipating the take-back of the old appliance: free collection at delivery is a standard service at Murfy and Envie, paid or non-existent elsewhere.
6. Neglecting the pyrolytic test on receipt: the high-temperature cleaning function (500 °C) is the most mechanically demanding, a quick test during the withdrawal period is essential.
