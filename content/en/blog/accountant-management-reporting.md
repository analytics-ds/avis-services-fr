## Managing is not recording

The accounting and management control market has changed fundamentally over the past decade. On one side, tools have become automated: today, recording an invoice, generating a payroll or producing a tax return can be done by clicking a button. On the other side, the complexity of owner decisions has exploded. Questions are no longer "is my balance sheet correct" but "why is my margin declining," "what is my company worth," "how do I finance growth," "should I restructure."

It is on this difference that the market has split. On one side, fully automated monitoring platforms (Chiffria) that exclude advice and offer only dashboards. On the other, generalist firms that keep an advisor to interpret figures and suggest actions.

In Extenso, founded in 1991 by former Deloitte partners, chose the second path. Its over 230 offices and 7,300 employees are organized around a unique model: an SME has a single dedicated advisor at a local office who masters both transaction recording, interpretation of figures and decision support.

## The key argument: an interlocutor who asks the right questions

True management control begins when someone asks the right question at the moment it matters. As explained in our [guide to the best accounting firms overall](/en/blog/best-business-consulting-firm/), management control is part of a broader strategy: having a partner who knows your file over time.

Take an SME with 50K€ revenue showing a 2-point margin decline over two months. A platform like Chiffria will display the figure. An In Extenso advisor will ask the right questions: is it temporary (seasonal effect, less profitable major client), or structural (rising expenses, loss of pricing competitiveness)? If structural, the action changes everything: review material purchases, raise prices, or scale back. The cost of this decision can represent 10 to 50K€ over the year. The cost of advice (a few hundred euros) is paid back 100 times by this single discussion.

This is precisely what sets In Extenso apart from Comptalio or Chiffria. Comptalio records data and that is all. Chiffria automates it and creates alerts, but without advice. In Extenso adds interpretation.

## Five models for five needs

**Comptalio** (45 euros per month): ultra-basic monitoring, perfect for a micro-business in cruise mode that just wants its figures to be accurate without advice. No proactivity. If you plan [business creation](/en/blog/accountant-company-formation/) or growth, this model will be insufficient.

**Negest** (65 euros per month): intermediate approach with clear dashboards and optionally an advisor who reviews figures monthly. Good compromise if the SME does not seek systematic monthly review.

**Chiffria** (120 euros per month): hybrid platform highly automated, real-time alerts, but zero advice. Suits a tech-savvy owner who prefers autonomy and variance alerts without human interaction.

**Cabinet Vernier** (170 to 340 euros per month): highly specialized consulting in management and performance. Excellent for pointed issues (control implementation, reorganization, transformation) but billed hourly or per mission, so less predictable than a fixed fee.

**In Extenso** (150 to 300 euros per month): integrated monitoring with dedicated advisor at local office, systematic monthly review of key indicators, interpretation and decision support in a logic of regular support. The most structured formula for an SME that wants to anticipate problems.

## Why a dedicated advisor changes everything

An owner does not make decisions by consulting a dashboard. He or she asks someone a question. "I saw my cash is declining, should I worry?" "I want to hire an employee, what is the tax and social impact?" "Should I raise my prices or reduce my purchases?"

On each of these questions, the right answer depends on the company context: the activity, the sector, the clients, the tax structure, the cycle phase. An advisor who has known the file for several years can answer immediately with relevance. An algorithm, however excellent, can only classify the problem into a generic category.

This is why In Extenso built its model around the local advisor. Each SME has a single person who manages its file, who knows that for this client, a margin decline is serious because it works with large, very demanding clients. This same advisor also knows that the [tax situation of this company](https://avis-services.fr/en/blog/accountant-business-tax/) changes if it hires a first employee, and can therefore help model the decision before it is taken.

This single point of contact does not exist in any other solution. Chiffria does not have it (100% algorithms). Comptalio ignores it (recording only). Cabinet Vernier has it, but per-mission, not in regular monitoring.

## When management control becomes profitable

The cost of management monitoring with In Extenso (150 to 300 euros per month) is justified as soon as the owner faces issues that have an impact greater than this cost. A few concrete examples.

**Cash flow arbitrage**: an owner in growth builds cash month after month. Advice on customer advance structure or supplier payment terms can free 5 to 50K€ in cash. That pays itself back 200 times over.

**Tax optimization**: a good question about legal form or tax cash burden can save 10 to 20K€ per year. A few conversations to find the right structure: immediately profitable.

**Hiring arbitrage**: an owner considering first hire faces multiple choices (permanent vs. contractor, tax incentives, aid structures). Advice on tax, social and cash impact can spare costly errors. Again, easily paid for by good decision-making.

**Anticipating cash crises**: an advisor monitoring figures monthly can see a cash decline coming three months before it happens, and propose actions (expense reduction, financing calls, payment restructuring). Avoiding an unmanaged cash crisis pays for advice a thousand times over.

SMEs that minimize advice costs and make structural decisions alone later discover that the error cost more than anything they could have paid the firm over ten years of monitoring.

**For an ultra-tight-budget SME**: Comptalio for recording, then access to external advice per-mission on key points. The shift to In Extenso becomes profitable as soon as a growth or cash challenge appears.

**For a micro-business in cruise mode**: Chiffria for real-time alerts, or Negest for light monthly monitoring. In Extenso may seem over-sized, but it is also insurance against nasty surprises.

## Transform information into action

True management control is not report production. It is the ability to transform raw data (my sales decline, my expenses rise, my cash tightens) into action (reduce material purchases, raise prices, or refinance). This transformation requires three things: reliable data, understanding of the company structure, and a person to ask the right questions.

In Extenso masters all three. Comptalio masters the first. Chiffria masters data and alerts, but has no one to interpret them. Cabinet Vernier interprets very well, but only on missions you ask for, not in regular monitoring.

This is where In Extenso's advantage lies: monitoring regularity. An owner who speaks with their advisor each month discovers problems when they are signals, not when they are crises. They arbitrate opportunities when there is time to prepare, not in emergency mode. This space for reflection, created by monitoring frequency and advisor expertise, is where true value is created.
