The phrase **private spa** guarantees very little in London. On sales pages it covers both the hire of an entire spa and a closed treatment room inside a venue that stays open to the public. The price gap between the two exceeds 300 pounds. This 2026 comparison sets out who genuinely books out a space in the British capital, at which address and at what price.

> **At a glance:**
> 1. Only two operators rent out a whole spa in London: Savannah Private Spa at 758 Harrow Road (NW10), and The Private Spa, with sites in Hackney Wick (E20) and Acton (W3). Their packages run from 150 to 249 pounds, with no therapist, for two to eight guests.
> 2. On the hotel side, only two addresses can be privatised through online booking, both five star and both in zone 1: Sofitel London St James at 300 pounds for two and InterContinental Park Lane at 550 pounds for two, both in the Sezame catalogue.
> 3. Spabreaks.com, Buyagift and Virgin Experience Days sell no privatisation. Their listings labelled private refer to treatment rooms, priced between 30 and 99 pounds per person.
> 4. Exclusivity is expensive once measured by the hour: 150 pounds for two hours of a whole spa at Savannah, against 40 pounds per person for three hours of access to a central five star hotel spa.

One dividing line runs through the table. Booking out an entire space does exist in London, but it comes down to three providers, and only one of them offers it inside a luxury hotel in the centre.

## Savannah Private Spa and The Private Spa: the two operators renting a whole spa

Savannah Private Spa occupies a site at 758 Harrow Road, postcode NW10, between Kensal Green and Willesden. It holds a heated pool with a built in jacuzzi, a steam room, a marble hammam table, a relaxation area and private facilities. Packages range from 150 to 249 pounds: Basic Spa for two to five guests, Romantic Spa for 2 from 199 pounds for two hours, Romantic Deluxe, Party Spa and Royal Hammam.

The Private Spa runs a similar model across two sites, one in Acton to the west, the other in Hackney Wick at 3 Copper Street, postcode E20, next to the Olympic park. Each suite takes two to eight guests and combines a Finnish sauna, steam room, plunge pool, bucket shower and experience showers. Wireless audio lets guests play their own music, and alcohol free food and drink can be ordered straight into the booked space. No rates appear on the site, with booking handled slot by slot and suite by suite.

Both addresses share one decisive feature that comparison pieces rarely flag: neither is central. Allow 25 to 40 minutes of travel from Oxford Circus. The reason is economic rather than editorial, since booking out a complete space requires floor area that West End rents rule out.

## The only two hotel spas in central London you can privatise

Filtering the London catalogue on privatisation returns exactly two bookable offers in August 2026.

Sofitel London St James, a five star a short walk from Trafalgar Square, lists Spa, Treatment and Champagne for Two at 300 pounds for two guests. It covers a treatment, steam room access and a glass of champagne, with the space held for the couple throughout the slot.

InterContinental Park Lane, five star facing Hyde Park Corner, lists The Wellness Temple at 550 pounds for two. It is the highest price in the London catalogue and buys privatisation of a complete treatment area in one of the best located hotels in the city.

Beyond these two addresses, privatising a London hotel spa is a matter of direct negotiation. The houses that dominate search results, Claridge's, the Guerlain Spa at Raffles London, Four Seasons Park Lane and Corinthia, handle such requests by phone, without public rates and over several days.

## Sezame: London hotel spas without the overnight stay

Sezame sells access to 4 and 5 star hotel spas to guests who are not booking a room. The London catalogue held 41 experiences in August 2026 across roughly a dozen properties, 23 of them including steam room access and 21 bookable for two. The listed price is the price paid, the date and slot are chosen at booking, and confirmation is immediate.

The addresses cluster in the centre: Hotel Café Royal on Regent Street, Montcalm Mayfair, Sofitel London St James, InterContinental Park Lane, Montcalm Royal London House and Montcalm East in Shoreditch, Hilton London Bankside on the South Bank, Courthouse Hotel Shoreditch and Leonardo Royal Tower Bridge. Only one address genuinely leaves the city, Hilton Syon Park in Brentford.

Entry pricing starts at 40 pounds per person for three hours of spa access at Montcalm Royal London House, which sets a benchmark for the hourly cost of exclusivity against shared access. Packages for two run from 199 pounds at Hotel Café Royal to 300 pounds at Montcalm Mayfair. The full catalogue sits at [joinsezame.com](https://joinsezame.com).

## Gift boxes and platforms: why privatisation is absent

Spabreaks.com lists 74 venues under its London heading, from 30 pounds per person. None of those listings covers hire of an entire space. The model rests on spa days in shared access, with or without a treatment.

Buyagift runs a Luxury Spas in London range priced between 39.99 and 99 pounds, and Virgin Experience Days carries around fifty products for two in London with a median near 162 pounds. Both sell a voucher to be converted into a booking later, which makes privatisation structurally impossible: exclusive use of a space requires a slot blocked in advance, which an open dated voucher cannot deliver.

The word private still appears across those catalogues. There it denotes a closed treatment room, standard across the industry. The quickest check is to look for a maximum capacity and a slot duration in the product page, since their absence signals a non private offer. The wider question is covered in the comparison of sites to [book a private spa](/en/blog/private-spa-booking-site/).

## Three checks before booking

The first is the postcode. Prefixes W, EC, SE1, SW1 and WC mean central London, NW10, E20 and W3 mean 25 to 40 minutes of travel, and TW or the prefixes of neighbouring counties mean a train journey.

The second is the stated capacity. A genuinely private offer always specifies a maximum number of guests, between two and eight depending on the operator. Its absence from the product page points to a treatment room.

The third is what the price contains. Champagne, lunch, robe hire and thermal access sometimes sit inside the rate and sometimes appear on arrival. On a 300 pound booking for two, an extra charged on site changes the comparison. The same care applies when buying a [luxury spa gift card](/en/blog/luxury-spa-gift-card/), where validity runs from one year to more than three depending on the issuer.

## How this comparison was built

Prices were recorded in August 2026 directly from operator websites and platform catalogues, in public display and without a customer account. Addresses and postcodes come from the venues' own contact pages. Capacities match the official figures stated on product pages.

Market context draws on the State of the Spa Industry Snapshot Survey 2024 published by the UK Spa Association, which found that 60% of surveyed British spas reported growth in day guest numbers over the previous twelve months. That rising demand for access without an overnight stay partly explains the elastic commercial vocabulary seen across sales pages.

Offers had to be bookable online by a private customer to qualify. Privately negotiated privatisations, which several London palace hotels do arrange, therefore fall outside the scope for lack of verifiable pricing.
